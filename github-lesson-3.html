1. Three ways to get code off GitHub
WayWhat you getCan you push back?
CloneEvery file plus the whole history, connected to the repoYes
Download ZIPA plain folder of files, no history, no connectionNo
View on github.comRead the code in your browserNo

If you plan to change the code, clone it. A ZIP is a dead end.

2. How to clone a repo
  1. In GitHub Desktop, click File, then Clone repository.
  2. Stay on the GitHub.com tab. The URL tab is for pasting a link to a repo that is not in your list.
  3. Click the repo you want from the list.
  4. Next to Local Path, click Choose and pick where the folder should land. Write that location down.
  5. Click Clone.

A repo someone invited you to only appears in this list after you accept the invitation.

3. Two computers, one repo

Each computer has its own copy. Nothing moves on its own.

ActionWhat it does
CloneBrings the repo down to a computer for the first time, with all its history.
CommitSaves a snapshot on that computer only.
PushSends commits up from that computer to GitHub.
PullBrings commits down from GitHub to that computer.

So work moves up with push and down with pull. If you committed at school and nothing shows up at home, the home computer needs to pull.

Push your work before you pull, and pull before you start working. What happens when two people change the same thing is Lesson 4.

4. Public or private
PrivatePublic
Who can find itYou and people you inviteAnyone on the internet
Who can clone itYou and people you inviteAnyone
Who can push to itYou and collaborators you inviteYou and collaborators you invite
Good forClass work and anything unfinishedShowing work to a college or employer later

Change it in repo Settings, then General, then scroll to the Danger Zone and Change repository visibility.

Before making anything public: no full names, no ID numbers, no passwords or keys in the code, and nothing you do not have the rights to share. Going back to private does not un-share what someone already copied.

A collaborator can push to your repo, which means they can change and delete your files. Only add people you actually work with.

6. Inviting a collaborator
  1. Ask for the person's exact GitHub username and read it back to them.
  2. On github.com, open your repo and click Settings.
  3. In the Access section of the sidebar, click Collaborators.
  4. Click Add people.
  5. Start typing the username and click the right person in the list of matches.
  6. Click the Add button that names the person and your repo. They show as Pending Invite.
  7. They accept from the email GitHub sends, or at github.com/notifications.

Invitations expire after 7 days. To take access away later, use Remove on the same Collaborators page.

7. Troubleshooting
ProblemFix
I never got the invitationCheck github.com/notifications or the email on your GitHub account. Make sure your partner typed your exact username.
Their repo is not in my clone listYou have not accepted the invitation yet. Accept it, then reopen the Clone window.
I already have a folder with that namePick a different Local Path or rename the old folder. Two repos cannot share one folder.
I cannot find the Settings tabYou only see Settings on repos you own. If the tabs are cut off, open the dropdown at the end of them.
8. Quick check

1. You want to work on your game from a different computer. What do you do?

Show answer

Clone the repo. You get every file and the history, and it stays connected so you can push back.

2. Your repo is private. Can your teacher open it?

Show answer

Only if you add them as a collaborator. Private means you and the people you invite.

3. You invited your partner an hour ago and they still cannot push. Why?

Show answer

They have not accepted the invitation yet.

4. You pushed from the lab computer. What does your home laptop need?

Show answer

A pull. Push sends work up, pull brings work down.

5. Your repo is public. Can a stranger change your code?

Show answer

No. They can read and clone it, but pushing to your repo takes a collaborator invitation from you.
